Why This Investigation

Why "The American Supplement Investigation"?

Nearly three in four American adults take a dietary supplement. Most assume — reasonably, given how similar the packaging looks to regulated medicine — that some level of government testing stands behind the claim on the label. In most cases, none does.

This is not an argument that supplements are fraudulent. Some work, for some people, for some purposes, and the evidence for a few is genuinely strong. It is an argument that the confidence with which a product is chosen and the evidence that actually exists for it are, far too often, two very different things.

"I write as an investigator, not an advocate. The most dangerous claims are rarely the obviously false ones. They're the ones that are technically true, biologically plausible, and still misleading."

Drawing on Joy Meier's clinical pharmacy expertise and Robert Coleman's forty years in medicine — sixteen as Director of Clinical Pharmacy Services at the VA Palo Alto Health Care System — this investigation applies the same evidence-based standard as Broken Promises Healthcare to a $60 billion industry most Americans engage with every single day.

How We Source Claims

Every Figure, Sourced

Every factual claim in this investigation is sourced — to peer-reviewed research, federal regulatory documents, or government data. Where a figure is an estimate or a range, we say so. If you believe a citation has been misrepresented, tell us — we want to know.
